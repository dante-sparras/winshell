# Screens keep their physical corner in logical coordinates

A screen's logical rectangle starts at its physical top-left corner, and its size is its physical size divided by its scale. That is what Qt does on Windows. Dividing each monitor's whole rectangle by its own scale, as winshell did first, makes monitors of mixed DPI overlap: a 100% monitor at 0 to 1920 and a 150% monitor to its right come out as 0 to 1920 and 1280 to 2560. Then a point on the desktop can belong to two screens, and a popup cannot be mapped back to physical pixels. With the corner kept, screens never overlap, but a scaled screen leaves a gap after it: the 150% monitor spans 1920 to about 3627. Positions are only meaningful within one screen, so a window, a popup, or a menu maps through the screen it is on.
